Measured 2026-09-29 (bl-65dc, wifi, engine 0.0.74 with the standing acceptor, phone build with bl-c21d): on return to the foreground the phone re-punched from exactly the port its landed call named, SYNs left the phone for 35 s, and NOTHING arrived at the engine's kernel (no SYN-RECV, no row on the punch port) — the engine's home NAT holds no mapping once the served stream has ended (~36 s earlier here), and the engine sends no SYNs outside a call's window, so a re-punch is one-sided. §13.3's premise 'a NAT mapping that outlives the window' does not hold for TCP on a home NAT. Ruling to record: (1) rung 3 as written is retired; its replacement is a RE-CALL: with cached presence (endpoints + the engine's punch port) the client writes a fresh call (one DHT walk, not two) and punches — ~8 s cheaper than a full rendezvous, and it is what every client already falls to today after the failed re-punch; the presence cache is invalidated by a call that expires unanswered. (2) The held connection with its 25 s pings is the ONLY thing that keeps the NAT mapping alive, so keeping it alive is the real fast-return path (yog-android bl-f677's ruling; lernie/thrall keep theirs). (3) The engine's standing acceptor (bl-5276) stays: it serves a client whose NAT-side mapping is still live and any client that can reach the punch port directly. Amend §13.3 (rungs: held line → direct → re-call → full rendezvous), §13.4, §13.8 (the measurement), DESIGN §12 if a file changes; the engine needs no code.
