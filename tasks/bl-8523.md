Round-1 install lane (STORIES S0/S9), at the door bl-2291 seated.

## Gesture and reply

    $ lernie start home "Say hello"
    {"kind":"prepared","ok":true,…}
    {"error":"sign in first: no provider in this workspace's wall holds a credential, so a conversation begun here would reach no model — /login <provider> (rows: anthropic, openai, mistral, openai-responses, google, ollama, claude-code, openai-chatgpt)","ok":false}

## Two defects in that one sentence

**1. Two of the eight rows have nothing to log in, and the refusal offers them
anyway.** `lernie providers home` on the same wall:

    ollama          auth none · no credential needed   blocked=keyless — nothing to log in
    claude-code     auth none · no credential needed   blocked=keyless — nothing to log in

A user who reads the refusal literally and types `/login claude-code` is told
there is nothing to log in — a loop with no exit.

**2. The remedy for a keyless row exists and is not named.**
`src/boundary/dispatch/signin.rs` states the predicate:

> A keyless row does not ready the wall by itself … **Unless the lineage the
> fire forks off names it in a role.** A role pointing at a keyless row is the
> operator's own hand, not the merge

So the act that unblocks a keyless setup is `lernie model <ws> <role>
<provider> <model>`, and the refusal never says the word "role". It says
`/login <provider>` and nothing else.

**And for one of the two rows that path is closed too:**

    $ lernie model home worker claude-code claude-sonnet-4-5
    {"error":"provider row `claude-code` cannot serve a role: claude_code declares
     no tools — every yog turn carries at least the `clients` tool, so this row can
     serve no role — pick a tool-capable row","ok":false}

So `claude-code` is listed by the refusal, cannot be logged in, and cannot be
named in a role. It cannot ready a wall by any act at all. That leaves `ollama`
as the only keyless row a fresh box can actually use, and it needs a local
server the suite does not mention.

## Expected

The refusal should partition the rows it prints by what act each one takes:
sign in (the credentialed protocols), assign to a role (the keyless rows that
can serve one), or nothing (the rows that cannot serve). The information is all
present at the site — `ProviderRow` carries `credential` and `tools`, and
`ready()` already reads both halves of the predicate.

## Severity

p2. Not p1 because a user with an API key gets past it, but it is the last gate
before a first reply and it is the one place the product tells a stranger what
to do next. Getting sent to a login that does not exist, for a provider that
could not have worked, is worse than a bare refusal.

Cross-reference for triage: bl-21e9 (p3) is adjacent and may be the same fix, but the two claims are different and both were sighted this round.

bl-21e9 is about WHICH ROWS a newborn wall has — brazen built-ins only, so the refusal points at eight rows and not at the config-defined one the roles name.

This ball is about the rows the refusal DOES print being unactionable: two of the eight are keyless (`blocked=keyless — nothing to log in`), so `/login` on either is a loop with no exit, and one of those two — `claude-code` — cannot be named in a role either (`claude_code declares no tools … this row can serve no role`). So it is offered, cannot be signed in to, and can never ready a wall by any act at all.

If a single fix partitions the printed rows by the act each one takes — sign in / assign to a role / cannot serve — it closes both.
