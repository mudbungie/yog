Follow-up to bl-56af, which removed lernie's whole-tree `budgets:` ceiling from
every workspace's `config/default` at the §8.6 workflow fixed point. That
converges the workspaces; it does not revive what the ceiling already killed.

## What is left standing

lernie freezes a branch's config at the commit it forked off, and re-reads the
frozen `budgets:` at **every model-call boundary**. So a conversation started
before the convergence keeps the ceiling for its whole life — no amount of
config advancing reaches it. When it crosses, lernie writes
`refs/lernie/budget-exhausted/<agent-id>` and treats it as an ordinary terminal
state: `prompt/dispatch/terminal.rs` parks the branch and deliberately does not
re-deposit against it.

Two consequences the operator meets, not one:

1. Every already-marked conversation is permanently dead. A long-lived
   workspace holds dozens of these marks, and the newest of them are from the
   day bl-56af was filed — a root plus three subagents, all four marked inside
   four minutes of wall.
2. Deleting the mark would not help. The frozen ceiling is still in that
   branch's config, the derived spend is still over it, so the very next model
   call re-marks it. There is no in-place resume.

## The question this ball is for

The escape hatch that plausibly already exists is the V2 **fork**: a fork
started after the convergence should freeze against the *current* config tip
and therefore be unbounded, carrying the transcript across while leaving the
dead branch dead. If that is what fork does, nothing needs building and this
ball is a §11 wording change — the budget wound should say what the way out is,
the way §8.6's park says what answering it does. If fork instead inherits the
parent's frozen config commit, then a conversation killed by a ceiling that no
longer exists has no path forward at all, and that is a real gap.

**Verify before designing.** Read what `lernie dispatch --from` / the fork path
actually freezes, and what yog's rail records as *config-frozen-at* for a
forked child (DESIGN §5.1 #31, §11). Do not assume either answer.

## Not in scope

Reviving a branch by deleting the mark. The mark is lernie's terminal state,
not yog's — yog is a pure reader of `refs/lernie/*` (§5.1 #14) and writing one
would be a second authority over a fact lernie owns.
