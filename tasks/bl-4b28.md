## Evidence

`$XDG_STATE_HOME/yog/ops.jsonl` (world state root) holds 4472 rows. 4447 of
them are:

    {"argv":["yog-drift","late"],"cwd":"<world>/state/yog","exit":-4,
     "origin":"world","stderr":"<world>/state/yog (3 s pass)\n","ts":"..."}

One every ~15 s (the full-sweep period), unbroken from 2026-08-12 20:32:30 to
now. The remaining 25 rows are the operator's whole real history: 17 `lernie`
verbs, 7 detached prompts, 1 `yog-step`.

`app::drift::lateness` fires when a pass takes at or past `Cadence::late_pass`
(the cheap-sweep period). Every pass takes 3-4 s. So the §7.2 instrumentation
is not reporting an anomaly — it is reporting the steady state, and the §11
activity accessory's drift count is a constant the operator cannot act on.

## Two things to separate

1. **Why passes take 3-4 s.** `workspaces/dev` carries 110 agent branches and
   110 step trees. If the derivation cost is linear in dead conversations, the
   cadence is wrong for a real workspace, not for this one.
2. **What a permanently-late derivation should write.** A drift line per sweep
   turns the durable trail into a log file. The signal wanted is "passes stopped
   keeping cadence at T", not 4447 restatements.

## Verdict: the code, not the environment (Jib)

**Two concurrent instances are not implicated.** Measured over the trail's 5067 late rows:
no second of wall clock carries two of them (a second deriving worker would have collided
somewhere in 5067 draws), and the inter-row gap is a single writer's 15 s cadence with
jitter — 4377 gaps of exactly 15 s, 258 of 16, 199 of 14, and no 7/8 s cluster that two
interleaved 15 s writers would leave. The '20:32:30 start' is also not the second
instance's birth: the row before it is the operator's own `yog-step clear-trail` at
20:32:27, so the trail simply has no history older than the truncation.

**The real cause: every pass was judged against the wrong promise.** `Cadence::late_pass`
returned the *cheap*-sweep period (2 s) for every pass, whatever it did. The rows land one
per 15 s — the FULL-sweep tick — because only a full sweep exceeded it: that pass
re-enumerates, re-fetches every project's balls, re-reads the ops tail and re-derives every
workspace, which in a real workspace (110 agent branches) costs 2-3 s. 3914 of the 5067 rows
say '2 s pass' and 1023 say '3 s' — i.e. the storm is the schedule working, reported as an
anomaly against a period that pass never owed. A full sweep is now held to the full-sweep
period; a cheap pass, and one that swept nothing, still owe the 2 s poll cadence.

**And late is now an edge, not a level.** The row is written on the pass that stops keeping
cadence and not again until one keeps it, so a permanently-late derivation is one dated
event rather than 4447 restatements. Nothing records the recovery: whether passes are late
*now* is already the §11 staleness line's derived answer (snapshot age vs two full sweeps),
and a state belongs to a query, an event to the trail.

Not fixed, deliberately: the 2-3 s full sweep itself. It is linear in branches and it is the
pass that is *supposed* to be expensive; nothing here makes it slower or faster.
