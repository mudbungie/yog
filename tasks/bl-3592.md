The same eight are rows of the roster and of the window's conversation list. One workspace shows `7 conversations 7 waiting` for a single goal the operator asked for.

STORIES S6 step 1 says the strip answers "does anything need me?". Nothing needs the operator about a compactor: it is the engine's own context-economy machinery, dispatched by `workflow.yaml`'s `worker_flush: dispatch(compactor)`, and "came to rest — your turn" is a sentence about a conversation an operator started. Its arrival on the queue is what S6 step 2 forbids in spirit — "a stall you can dismiss is a stall you will miss" cuts both ways: a queue that is two thirds machinery is a queue nobody reads.

The rows are distinguishable off `preview` alone (`You are the compactor for branch ...`), which is exactly the sort of string-sniffing the derivation should not need — the compactor's role is a fact of the config, not of its opening line.

Reproduction: run any conversation long enough to pass the shipped `every_n_commits: 20` checkpoint, then `lernie attention`.

Expected: a conversation dispatched by the harness itself (`compactor`, and any future engine-owned role) does not stir the attention strip and does not occupy a roster row at the operator's altitude. Its failures still must reach somebody — the ops trail is where a machine's failure belongs.

Severity p2: it does not lose work, but it destroys the surface S6 and S14 both exist to provide. With three goals in flight the queue is unusable.
