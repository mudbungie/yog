Found while driving S0/S1 for bl-bf79 on 2026-08-13. Not a yog defect: a
**harness** defect, and the S0 payoff beat has been structurally red since the
2026-08-11 blast-radius ruling.

## The evidence

Two independent runs of `make drive DRIVE_RUNS=run`, one on the pre-fix binary
and one on the fixed one, both report:

    S0 payoff: wire reply on disk                  FAIL — no gpt reply in 40s

The first step of the first conversation, verbatim
(`steps/<agent>/001/response.json`):

    {"type":"error","kind":"config","message":"unknown provider `openai-chatgpt`","provider_detail":null}
    {"type":"end"}

## Root cause

`scripts/drive/stories.sh run()` cannot seed the wall in time, and the ordering
is forced, not sloppy:

    read -r pid wid < <("$drive" launch "$data")
    ... bare_start   # ONE gesture: `lernie new` AND the detached `lernie prompt`
    ws_root=$(find "$data/yog/workspaces" ...)
    seed_wall "$data" "$ws_root"

`harness.sh`'s own comment already names the trap and stops one step short of
its consequence:

    # The wall is keyed by the workspace's §3.1 leaf, and yog MINTS that leaf at
    # the fire, so the fixture cannot be laid before the workspace exists: this
    # runs after the mint.

The mint and the first `lernie prompt` are the SAME gesture (one Enter fires
both — the ops trail shows `lernie new`, `lernie config … default`, then
`lernie prompt` within the same second). So the wall is necessarily empty when
the first driver makes its first model call, and DESIGN §16.2 is explicit that
this is correct product behaviour, not a bug:

> **A wall is born empty, and its rows arrive after birth (bl-00ee).** It is
> keyed by the §3.1 leaf, and that leaf is minted *by* the birth, so nothing can
> seed a wall before the workspace it walls exists. A newborn workspace
> therefore answers brazen's shipped rows and nothing else.

`openai-chatgpt` is not a shipped row — `make drive-preflight` says so in as
many words: `openai-chatgpt  VIA CFG  not a shipped row — it reaches the wall
only through config.toml above`. So the first turn of every scratch-world run
is *guaranteed* to fail while the beat asserting it is a wire beat.

The second turn succeeds, because `seed_wall` has landed by then — which is how
bl-bf79's revive beat was drivable at all.

## Why nobody saw it

`docs/drive-logs/2026-08-07-ladder-rebaseline.md` has this beat PASS. That run
predates the 2026-08-11 ruling that moved brazen inside the wall. Nothing has
driven S0/S1 between the ruling and 2026-08-13, so the red has been latent for
two days and would have been read as a wire outage by the next driver.

## Attack the fix before taking it

Three candidates, none obviously right:

1. **Seed the wall against a name yog will mint.** Impossible by construction —
   that is the §3.1 mint, and §16.2 above says so.
2. **Two-gesture S0**: mint a workspace (`w`, §11's new-workspace binding),
   seed its wall, THEN start a conversation in it. Costs the "bare start" beat
   its bareness — S0's whole subject is launch→type→Enter with no prior
   workspace — so it may be trading the rung's meaning for a green light.
3. **Make the beat honest instead**: S0's payoff on a *newborn* workspace is
   brazen's shipped rows, so the wire beat belongs on the SECOND turn (which is
   what bl-bf79's revive beat now asserts) and S0's payoff should assert the
   rendered refusal, not a reply. This is the cheapest and possibly the truest —
   but it retires a wire beat, so it needs a ruling, not a patch.

Related: bl-55d8 (a step that dies before its first token should render its
reason). If that lands, option 3 has a surface to assert against.
