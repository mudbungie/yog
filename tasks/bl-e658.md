**yog twin of thrall's bl-9261**, filed here under the fix-where-it-lives
doctrine: the finding was made driving a foot, and the remedy is entirely in
this crate's mailbox (`src/registry/mailbox/slots.rs`).

## The defect

`Mailbox::drain` sets `taken = true` at the moment an invocation is handed to
the *answering code*, not at the moment the client received it:

    for slot in slots.live.values_mut() {
        if slot.client == client && !slot.taken {
            slot.taken = true;
            out.push(slot.invocation.clone());
        }
    }

`taken` is a latch: nothing un-takes it, nothing acknowledges it, and no later
`take` offers it again. `Mailbox::take` polls `drain` for `HOLD_WAITS *
HOLD_TICK` — 240 looks 125 ms apart, thirty seconds — and a thread sleeping in
that loop has not learned that its peer's socket is gone. So an invocation
posted into a **parked read whose connection has already died** is consumed by
a thread that cannot deliver it, and is never redelivered.

## Proven by a discriminating pair

- Stop the foot, post an invocation about two seconds later, restart it: the
  foot comes up, re-presents, serves new work immediately, and the one posted
  during the window never arrives and never earns a capture.
- Stop the foot, wait past the thirty-second hold, post the same invocation,
  restart it: delivered and answered normally.

The two runs differ in nothing but the wait.

## What a reader sees

Nothing. `capture` answers absent, and absent is documented as *still running*
so that a reader never has to tell "not finished" from "finished saying
nothing". A lost invocation is therefore indistinguishable from a slow one for
the full hour of the slot's TTL.

## The window is routine

Thirty seconds is long and the events that land in it are ordinary: a foot
restarting under its supervisor, a network blip, a box being upgraded, and
every case where the foot dies mid-invocation.

## Shape of the remedy

`taken` wants to be a **lease**, not a latch. The connection is not the lease's
scope — a foot dials per ask and drops the connection the moment the answer is
read, so a lease released on connection end would re-queue every invocation
the instant it was correctly delivered. The honest acknowledgement inside the
existing shapes is the client's **next follow-class read**: a foot's loop is
serial (`invocations -> hand off -> complete`, one at a time), so a client
asking for work again while a slot it was handed carries no capture is the
client saying it did not finish that slot.

Redelivery makes the leg at-least-once where it was silently at-most-once.
The invocation id is stable across a redelivery, so it is the idempotency key
a far end can dedupe on, and REMOTE must say so rather than leaving it implied.

Safety of the predicate depends on there being one reader per client identity,
which is the sibling ball's ruling.
