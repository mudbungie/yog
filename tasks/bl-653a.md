bl-4263 landed the engine end; here is the contract this client mirrors, byte for byte (REMOTE §13.2 as amended). MATERIAL an entry holds: the engine rendezvous public key (32 bytes ed25519) and the pairing salt (32 bytes), beside ca.pem. DERIVATIONS, all HKDF-SHA256 with salt "yog rendezvous" over the pairing salt, one info label each, 32 bytes out: "presence salt" (the DHT salt presence is filed under), "inbox salt", "seal key" (ChaCha20-Poly1305), "inbox key" (an ed25519 SEED — the inbox is signed under a keypair derived from the salt, so the client mints no keypair of its own for it). PRESENCE: BEP 44 get(engine public key, presence salt); the value is nonce(12) || ciphertext || tag(16) under the seal key, plaintext = endpoint list: a count byte, then per endpoint a family byte (4 or 6), the raw address bytes (4 or 16), a big-endian u16 port. The engine publishes its route-local addresses at its punch port (the observed endpoint is bl-efae), republished hourly with a rising seq. CALL (the inbox): put(inbox keypair signs, inbox salt, seq rising per write, value sealed the same way) with plaintext = 8-byte big-endian nonce then an endpoint list — the client punch port at the addresses it can be reached on. The engine polls every 15 s, remembers the last nonce it punched, and punches ONLY a call that verifies and unseals; a fresh nonce is one punch. PUNCH: bind one port with SO_REUSEADDR+SO_REUSEPORT, listen on it per family, and connect from the same port toward every engine endpoint, v6 first, one SYN per 2 s for a 20 s window; the engine does the same toward the call endpoints. Keep the FIRST stream you get — the engine serves every stream that lands, so whichever one you speak on is answered. WIRE over the stream: the ordinary inner mTLS as a client (ca.pem, your leaf, verify the engine name), the §3 preface, request frames as always. HELD: between asks the engine writes {"ping":true} as a frame every 25 s of silence — discard it wherever you read, it is never the start of a reply stream — and hangs up after 2 min of total silence, so a client that wants the connection held answers nothing and simply stays.

Seat half landed in lernie as bl-65ee, squash 5441c3a on lernie main (DESIGN §4.40 is the record). What the seat now does: an entry reads two more files beside ca.pem — rendezvous.pub (the engine's 32-byte ed25519 rendezvous public key, hex) and pairing.salt (hex) — both absent is an entry with no roving, one absent is a refusal; the four HKDF derivations, the sealed-item format and a signed BEP 44 item are pinned to bytes yog's own material.rs/item.rs produced, so the two implementations are held equal by test rather than by reading. The seat carries its own bencode/KRPC/BEP 44 client (src/dht, ring only; the find_node walk dropped since a rendezvous never needs it) and socket2 =0.6.5 for the punch on the same terms yog got it. Every dial climbs held line, direct address (bounded only when material stands below it), re-punch at RAM-cached endpoints, full rendezvous (presence get, sealed call put under the derived inbox keypair with a fresh nonce and a rising seq, punch from one port bound once per run, first stream kept); the DHT is touched on the last rung only, and the same mTLS verifies the same name off the address whichever rung answered. A punched line is held between asks; pings are discarded wherever a frame is read; a held line is judged gone one ping before the two-minute bound on an injected clock, and before it is spoken on it is asked whether it is still open (FIN, close_notify, reset, non-TLS bytes, or buffered pings) so a dropped line re-enters the ladder rather than turning an act into doubt. Suite: each rung taken and each falling through against a fake DHT on loopback UDP and a stand-in engine serving a held line, the engine's own SYN landing on the seat's listener, the ping discard and the bound on a fake clock.
