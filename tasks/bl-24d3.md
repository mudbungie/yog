## Confirmed clean (glyph present in both families) — do not touch

`⚑ ⏭ ★ ▶ ⚙ ■ ⚠ ？ ○ • · ↙ ↻ × ≠ ≤ ✉ §`

## Why this one matters more than bl-4c0c did

`●` and `◐` are the **live** and **in-flight** badges in the §11 conversation
list — the primary at-a-glance signal for "is this thing running". `■`
(Stopped) and `○` render fine, so the operator sees real glyphs for the settled
states and boxes for the two active ones. That is worse than uniformly broken:
it reads as a distinct third state rather than as a missing font.

`✓`/`✗` are missing in **both** families, so the transcript and steps-view
good/error badges are tofu wherever they are painted.

## Fix direction — decide, do not guess

Two shapes, and they are not equivalent:

1. **Swap the codepoints** for ones the bundled font covers. Cheapest, no new
   dependency, keeps `make install` byte-identical. But it constrains the visual
   language to whatever egui happens to ship, and the §11 badge vocabulary was
   chosen deliberately (see `src/theme/mod.rs` `state_badge` and DESIGN §11).
2. **Ship a font** with the coverage and register it in the egui font
   definitions. Fixes the class of problem rather than these seven instances,
   and stops the next glyph anyone adds from silently tofu-ing. Costs binary
   size and a licensing decision (the crate is published to crates.io — check
   `deny.toml`'s licence policy before choosing a font).

AGENTS.md: "Dissolve a class of edge cases with one invariant, not an answer
apiece." That argues for (2). Weigh it honestly against the binary-size and
licence cost and say which you chose and why.

**Whichever you pick, add the guard**: a test that walks every non-ASCII char in
`src` string literals and asserts `has_glyph` in the family that will paint it.
Without that, this recurs the moment someone types a nice-looking arrow. That
guard is the real deliverable; the seven fixes are the occasion for it.
