Filed from bl-e266's second ask (the reachability investigation; full evidence in that ball's journal, delivered as ecf6136).

bl-e266 established that attention is NOT near-unreachable — all five signal rules fire, and pinned lernie 0.0.2 genuinely writes refs/lernie/{notify,budget-exhausted,conflicted}/<id> (prompt/workflow_actions.rs, prompt/budget/mod.rs, prompt/dispatch/transfer.rs). Yet the operator, live 2026-07-28, reported never seeing the strip say anything but "nothing stirs". Two structural reasons, both currently BY DESIGN, and both are design questions rather than defects. That is what this ball must settle.

## 1. A clean end is Quiescent, and Quiescent is not an attention signal

An agent that finishes its turn and waits for you never stirs the strip. So a session in which nothing goes wrong stirs nothing, forever — the strip is silent precisely when the work is healthy.

The question: does the strip answer "what went wrong / who called me?" (its current contract) or "whose turn is it?" (what the operator appears to expect)? These are different products. The first is an exception channel; the second is a work queue. Do not answer by adding a sixth signal rule to §5.1 — decide which question the surface owns, and let the rule set follow. If the answer is "both", say explicitly how one surface carries two questions without either drowning the other.

## 2. Attention self-clears on the gesture that reveals it

focus_agent -> record_seen stamps every present evidence oid, and it is reached from a conversation-row click (src/shell/conv_list.rs:116), a descent-member click (src/shell/workspace.rs:163), keyboard up/down, AND the jump control itself. So clicking a dead conversation acks it before the operator has looked at anything — the ack races the reveal.

The question: is "seen" the right predicate for clearing, or is it clearing on the wrong gesture? Note the jump control acking its own destination is the sharpest case: press next, arrive, and the reason you were sent there is already gone.

Ruled out as causes (do not re-investigate): the strip is global — every workspace gets a tree at AppModel::new — and startup focus picks a workspace only, never an agent, so it acks nothing at boot.
