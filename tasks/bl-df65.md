## The operator's ruling, verbatim (2026-07-27)

> workspace names: this is a design miss. workspaces need names, and they are
> explicit. on this box, they would roughly be "ops" and "dev", and possibly one
> for my current employer. conversations get names that are auto-generated by the
> system. the reason for this is because two agents working together without a
> discriminator will become confused at their own identity, or blame the operator
> or a parent. it's good to give any given context window its own name. I'm not
> entirely sure that the workspace should even be implicitly included in context;
> it's purely organizational principle for the user.

## What this overturns

bl-68d9 ruled by subtraction: no conversation name is minted, the mint stays
per-workspace and random (`<workspace>`, `<workspace>`), and the preamble was to
become `Your workspace is <name>.` Three of its four load-bearing claims are now
wrong:

1. **The workspace name is minted, random, and two-word.** §3.1: "The name is
   minted at creation: two words from an embedded wordlist, hyphenated… The
   mint is a pure function over an injected RNG and the occupied set."
   → Operator wants **explicit, chosen, short** names: `ops`, `dev`, an employer
   name. A random wordlist mint is the wrong mechanism for a thing the human
   names on purpose.
2. **Conversations get no name.** bl-68d9 §3.3 rejected per-conversation minted
   names as "a second namespace whose one job the first payload line already
   does."
   → Reversed with a reason bl-68d9 did not weigh: **agent self-identity**. Two
   agents collaborating without a discriminator confuse themselves for each
   other, or misattribute work to the operator or to a parent. The name is not
   a display label — it is what lets a context window know which one it is.
   The first payload line cannot do that job.
3. **The preamble states the workspace.** bl-2706 was filed to implement
   `Your workspace is <name>.`
   → The operator questions whether the workspace belongs in agent context at
   all: "purely organizational principle for the user." **Settle this.** If the
   sphere is organizational-only, the preamble names the conversation and the
   workspace never enters the prompt.

What survives from bl-68d9: its analysis of why the ball CLAIMANT cannot simply
move to the conversation — §8.1 claims before the prompt fires and never reads
the root id back, so a conversation-keyed claimant would name a thing that does
not exist at claim time. That constraint is real and still binds. Re-read it
before proposing a claimant change.

## What the design must answer

1. **How is a workspace named?** An explicit operator-supplied name at mint
   time. Then: what validates it (§3.1 currently guarantees path-safety by
   construction from a curated wordlist — an operator-typed name has no such
   guarantee); what happens on collision; what the empty-world bootstrap does
   when nobody has typed a name yet (§3.4 mints one today with no operator
   present); and whether the wordlist survives at all as a fallback.
2. **How is a conversation named?** Auto-generated, per the ruling. From what —
   the wordlist the workspace mint is vacating? Uniqueness scope: per workspace
   or global? What does the §11 conversation list render, and what happens to
   the root-id fallback?
3. **What is in the preamble?** The conversation's name certainly. The
   workspace's — decide, per the operator's open question, and say why.
4. **What carries `YOG_NAME` and the `bl` shim's `--as` default?** (§16.7 W9,
   `src/world/tools`.) §3.2's "a ball is bound to a workspace iff its claimant
   equals the workspace's name" is the invariant under pressure. If the
   workspace name becomes operator-chosen, the join still works — but the
   collision guarantees change. Chase it.
5. **Migration.** Existing workspaces on disk carry minted names (`<workspace>`,
   `<workspace>`, `native-album`, `<workspace>` on the operator's box).
   §3.1 makes the directory name the registration — "the name namespace *is* the
   readdir; no registry file." A rename is a directory move. Say what happens to
   them, and to any ball whose claimant matches the old name.
