Found by yog-android bl-97ed (0147629) while giving the phone a stable per-entry punch port: the engine's NAT keeps a mapping toward (client address, the port the call named), so a re-punch from that port reaches the engine's punch listener and the kernel completes the handshake — but src/wire/rendezvous/call.rs only iterates punch.punch(endpoints, window) for the call it is answering, and nothing accepts on the listener between calls. The client sees a connected socket with no opening frame. The phone now guards it (10 s preface bound, a spent re-punch falls to a fresh call), but rung 3 cannot work end to end until the engine reads what lands.

Deliver: the punch listeners (src/wire/rendezvous/punch.rs, v4 + v6, alive for the run) get an accept loop on the loop's own thread family that serves EVERY accepted stream through the same serve(stream, tls, answerer, presence, quiet) path a call's punch feeds — mTLS authenticates it (REMOTE §5, fail-closed), so an unsolicited stream is exactly as safe as an accepted one on the front door; a stream that fails the handshake is dropped with one log line (family only). The per-call punch keeps doing what it does (simultaneous open toward the call's endpoints); the accept loop is what makes rung 3 and a client's plain connect through a live NAT mapping both land. Stats gain 'accepted' beside 'served'; say.rs gains 'stream accepted on the punch port (v4) — served' said once per burst. Amend REMOTE §13.3 (rung 3's engine half: the engine serves what lands on its punch port at any time) and §13.4, DESIGN §12.
