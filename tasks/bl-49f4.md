The operator's ruling, 2026-07-27, verbatim:

> on reloads: 2s/cheap sweep and 15/full sweep? the idea is to design the system
> to never need this. go implement instrumentation on the sweeps, and simulate
> scenarios to demonstrate the real systems and processes that would result in a
> drift. prove them out, and in general, consider them bugs, because changes
> should be picked up automatically. I'd rather not have a self-healing system
> covering a real architectural degredation.

## What exists today

DESIGN §7.2 / `src/app/dirty.rs`:

    pub const DEBOUNCE:    Duration = Duration::from_millis(100);
    pub const CHEAP_SWEEP: Duration = Duration::from_secs(2);
    pub const FULL_SWEEP:  Duration = Duration::from_secs(15);

- inotify (`src/fs_watcher`, `notify::RecommendedWatcher`) marks roots dirty;
  a 100 ms window coalesces the storm.
- The 2 s cheap sweep reconciles the watch set and re-probes liveness.
- The 15 s full sweep re-derives **everything** and marks every workspace dirty.
  `src/app/derive.rs:121` states its purpose outright: *"so a dropped inotify
  event costs ≤15 s of latency, never divergence."*

That comment is the admission. The full sweep exists to paper over dropped
events. It works — and it hides both how often events are dropped and why.

## The ask, in three parts

### 1. Instrument

Make the sweeps report what they FOUND, not merely that they ran. The full
sweep already re-derives every workspace and `rederive` already compares
(`GitTree: PartialEq` suppresses no-op repaints, §7.2) — so the signal is
almost free: **a full sweep whose re-derivation changes a snapshot the watcher
never marked dirty is a dropped event, and it is measurable right there.**
Count it, attribute it (which root, which kind), and surface it. The same holds
for the cheap sweep's reconcile finding a workspace the enumeration roots did
not announce.

Per ~/AGENTS.md, do not add a stored counter if a query will do, and do not
build an index — this is instrumentation, not a metrics system. `ops.jsonl` and
the §11 activity accessory are the existing surfaces; prefer feeding them to
minting a fourth.

### 2. Simulate and prove

Build the scenarios that actually produce drift, as tests, not as prose:

- inotify watch-descriptor exhaustion (`fs.inotify.max_user_watches`) — the
  classic, and yog watches per workspace root.
- a workspace created/removed while the watch set is being reconciled.
- atomic-rename sequences (`git` writes refs this way constantly) — the watcher
  claims to coalesce these; prove it.
- a workspace on a filesystem inotify does not cover (NFS, overlayfs, a
  container bind mount) — `RecommendedWatcher` silently degrades to polling on
  some platforms and not others.
- `git gc` / pack rewrites under a watched `repo.git`.
- the nested-world roots (§16) — `LERNIE_HOME` and `XDG_STATE_HOME` are
  overridden under `$XDG_DATA_HOME/yog/world`; confirm every root that matters
  is actually watched and none is reached only by sweep.

For each: does the watcher catch it? If not, that is a **bug with a name**, and
it gets its own ball. The deliverable of this task is that list, proven.

### 3. Re-decide the sweeps

Once the drift sources are known and fixed, the sweeps should be justifiable on
their own terms or removed. The operator's position is that a change should be
picked up automatically and a sweep that catches something is evidence of a
defect. A sweep that provably catches nothing across the scenario suite is
either dead weight (delete it — AGENTS.md: subtraction) or a deliberate,
documented backstop whose catches are alarms rather than routine. Say which,
and write the reasoning into §7.2 — which today presents the 15 s sweep as a
latency bound rather than as a symptom.

Note the cheap sweep also does liveness re-probing, which is a poll of process
state, not of the filesystem — a different justification that may well survive
when the filesystem one does not. Separate them in the analysis.

## Acceptance

- A scenario suite that produces each drift source deliberately, with the
  watcher's behaviour asserted per scenario.
- Instrumentation that names a dropped event when one happens, reachable by the
  operator without a debugger.
- One ball filed per real defect found.
- §7.2 rewritten to say what the sweeps are FOR, given the evidence.

## Do not

Do not delete the sweeps before the scenarios prove them unnecessary. The
self-healing is currently load-bearing precisely because the defects are
unknown; removing the cover before fixing what it covers turns a hidden problem
into a visible outage.

## Outcome (scorched-49f4)

### 1. Instrumentation — what exists, and where it surfaces

**Provenance, not a metrics system.** Every dirty root now carries a
`watch::Mark` naming *why* it is dirty. Marks merge with `max` over a
weakest-explanation-first order — `Sweep < Poll < Watch < Desync` — so the
strongest available explanation always wins and a blanket sweep mark can never
mask a real announcement (nor the reverse).

| Mark | Set by | A re-derivation that CHANGES the snapshot means |
|---|---|---|
| `Watch` | an allowlisted watcher event (§7.1) | the watcher working |
| `Desync` | the backend announcing it LOST events | a real drop, **announced** — re-derived at once |
| `Poll` | the 2 s cheap sweep's liveness re-probe | the poll working (no fs event exists for a released flock) |
| `Sweep` | the 15 s blanket mark | **nobody announced it — a dropped event** |

That is the whole signal, and it is free: `rederive` already compares
(`GitTree: PartialEq`). `src/app/drift.rs` names three findings —
`Desync(root)`, `Unannounced(root)`, `Unenumerated(root)` — and folds a tick's
findings into `ops.jsonl` as **one line per kind**, `argv = ["yog-drift",
<kind>]`, roots newline-joined in `stderr`, `exit = -4` (`opslog::DRIFT_EXIT`).

**Surfaces (both pre-existing, no fourth minted):**

- `ops.jsonl` (§4.2) — the durable, two-instance-shared trail. Per-kind rather
  than per-root so a systematic drift source cannot flood the 256-line tail and
  bury its own evidence.
- The §11 activity accessory — `activity · N ops · M ⚠ · K drift`. `K` is a
  **query over the tail** (`rows.iter().filter(OpRow::drift).count()`), not a
  stored counter. A drift row is deliberately NOT `OpRow::failed`: it accuses
  the watcher, not the operator's last action, so it never hijacks the §7.3
  failure banner (`last_failure` is unaffected). The chip goes ichor on drift.

**No stored counters, no index, no new file.** A `Drift` lives for one tick.

### 2. Scenarios built, and what each showed

`src/fs_watcher/drift_tests.rs` (watcher layer) and `src/app/tests/drift.rs`
(model layer). Each is a test, none is prose.

| Scenario | Result | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| inotify queue overflow (`IN_Q_OVERFLOW`) | **BUG.** notify 6.1.1 forwards it as a rescan-flagged event with an EMPTY `paths` vec; yog's `for path in paths` ingest iterated zero times and the kernel's own loss announcement vanished | Fixed at the watcher layer |
| watch-descriptor exhaustion (`fs.inotify.max_user_watches`) mid-tree | **BUG.** notify sends `ErrorKind::MaxFilesWatch` on the *event* channel; yog matched `Ok(Err(_)) => {}` and discarded the only notification that a subtree had gone blind | Fixed at the watcher layer |
| exhaustion at arm time | `WatchSet::reconcile` skips and retries every 2 s. Deliberately NOT alarmed on: `replays/` and `balls/clones/` are routinely absent, so an "unarmed" alarm would be permanent noise. The *consequence* surfaces as `yog-drift unannounced` the moment anything there changes | By design, documented |
| workspace created / removed between reconciles | Caught, and now reported (`unenumerated`), keyed on the workspace-set **membership delta** so a finding is reported once — not re-accused every 2 s by a workspace that merely fails to derive | Instrumented |
| atomic-rename sequences (git ref writes: `<ref>.lock` → `<ref>`) | Already correct. Pinned with a real-git test: one `Touched` at the destination, the `.lock` source never leaks | No defect |
| `git gc` / `git pack-refs` | `pack-refs` itself is seen (loose refs are deleted under `repo.git/refs/`, allowlisted). But **deleting a ref that is only packed rewrites `repo.git/packed-refs` ALONE** and touches nothing under `repo.git/refs/` — while `git for-each-ref` (loose + packed) drops it from the derived tree. **BUG:** a reproducible, fully silent dropped event | Fixed: `repo.git/packed-refs` allowlisted |
| watched dir deleted + re-created (re-primed clone) | **BUG.** The backend watches an *inode*. §7.3 claimed the 2 s reconcile rebuilds it; it never did — the `(path, kind)` key is still in `desired`, so the name-keyed diff kept a deaf watcher forever | Fixed: `Watcher::is_stale` compares armed `(dev, ino)` vs current |
| startup read/arm ordering | **BUG.** `AppModel::new` derived every snapshot and armed the watches *after* — a window blind by construction, silently repaired 15 s later | Fixed: arm, then read |
| nested-world roots (§16) — is every root that matters watched? | **BUG, not fixed here.** `RootKind::BrazenConfig` and `RootKind::LernieConfig` are in the §7.1 table and implemented in `roots.rs`, but `desired_watches` never arms them and no `Roots` field even names their paths. The sweeps do not cover this — the full sweep does not re-read config either | Filed as **bl-9130** |
| filesystems inotify does not cover (NFS / overlayfs / bind mounts / FUSE) | Not simulatable without that infrastructure; genuinely OS-level | Residual, below |

### 3. Watcher-layer fixes made

1. `Watcher::tick` no longer discards backend losses. `drain()` (a new pure seam
   over the channel, so both loss arms are provable without pushing a kernel
   into overflow) returns "the backend announced a loss"; the loss surfaces as
   ONE `ChangeKind::Desynced` change **on the root itself**. That dissolves the
   special case — it is the ordinary whole-root re-derivation with the root as
   the changed path — and yields `Mark::Desync`, so the root re-derives
   immediately instead of waiting ≤15 s.
2. `repo.git/packed-refs` added to the Workspace allowlist.
3. `WatchSet::reconcile` drops a **stale** watcher (root gone or inode replaced)
   and re-arms it, making §7.3's long-standing claim true.
4. `AppModel::new` arms the watch set before taking the first snapshot.

Test-hermeticity fix, needed to run the gate at all: the git fixtures
(`src/git_tree/tests/git.rs`, `tests/support/mod.rs`, `tests/pluggability.rs`)
now spawn git with `GIT_CONFIG_GLOBAL=/dev/null` / `GIT_CONFIG_SYSTEM=/dev/null`.
The machine's ambient `core.hooksPath` policy hook was running against every
throwaway fixture repo and failing 66 tests. A test world that reads the
developer's config is not a test world.

### 4. The sweeps, re-decided (DESIGN §7.2 rewritten)

- **The 15 s full sweep stays — as a deliberate, documented backstop whose
  catches are ALARMS, not routine.** It is not deleted because residual drop
  sources are outside yog's reach (below). It no longer repairs silently.
- **The 2 s cheap sweep's liveness re-probe survives on its own terms** and is
  not a filesystem backstop at all: it polls *process* state, for which no
  filesystem event exists to drop. Its roots are marked `Poll` and its changes
  are never counted as drops. Its reconcile half IS filesystem-facing and is
  instrumented.
- §7.2 now carries a "What the sweeps are FOR" subsection with the Mark table,
  the residuals, and the fix list. §7.1 gains the `packed-refs` row and its
  rationale; §7.3's failure table is rewritten with the real handling.

### 5. Residual OS-level cases (not fixable at the watcher layer)

1. **Filesystems inotify does not cover** — NFS, overlayfs, container bind
   mounts, FUSE. `notify::RecommendedWatcher` arms successfully and then simply
   never fires for changes made outside the local kernel's view. There is no
   error, no rescan flag, nothing to detect. **The sweep is the only cover**, and
   this is the load-bearing reason it is not deleted. Repeated
   `yog-drift unannounced` lines on one workspace are now the operator's signal
   that the workspace sits on a filesystem yog cannot watch.
2. **notify's recursive watch-add race** — a directory tree created faster than
   the backend can add a watch to each new subdirectory loses the events that
   land inside it before its watch is armed. Not addressable above the library.
3. **Drift is a lower bound, not an exact count.** A workspace holding a
   Live/InFlight agent is marked `Poll` every 2 s, so a genuine drop there is
   explained away by the liveness poll. The measurement is exact on the 15 s
   full-sweep tick (which re-probes no liveness — `Full` supersedes `Cheap`) and
   for quiescent workspaces.

### 6. Balls filed

- **bl-9130** — the BrazenConfig / LernieConfig watch roots are in §7.1 but never
  armed; external config edits reach no yog surface, and no sweep covers it.
