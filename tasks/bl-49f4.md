The operator's ruling, 2026-07-27, verbatim:

> on reloads: 2s/cheap sweep and 15/full sweep? the idea is to design the system
> to never need this. go implement instrumentation on the sweeps, and simulate
> scenarios to demonstrate the real systems and processes that would result in a
> drift. prove them out, and in general, consider them bugs, because changes
> should be picked up automatically. I'd rather not have a self-healing system
> covering a real architectural degredation.

## What exists today

DESIGN §7.2 / `src/app/dirty.rs`:

    pub const DEBOUNCE:    Duration = Duration::from_millis(100);
    pub const CHEAP_SWEEP: Duration = Duration::from_secs(2);
    pub const FULL_SWEEP:  Duration = Duration::from_secs(15);

- inotify (`src/fs_watcher`, `notify::RecommendedWatcher`) marks roots dirty;
  a 100 ms window coalesces the storm.
- The 2 s cheap sweep reconciles the watch set and re-probes liveness.
- The 15 s full sweep re-derives **everything** and marks every workspace dirty.
  `src/app/derive.rs:121` states its purpose outright: *"so a dropped inotify
  event costs ≤15 s of latency, never divergence."*

That comment is the admission. The full sweep exists to paper over dropped
events. It works — and it hides both how often events are dropped and why.

## The ask, in three parts

### 1. Instrument

Make the sweeps report what they FOUND, not merely that they ran. The full
sweep already re-derives every workspace and `rederive` already compares
(`GitTree: PartialEq` suppresses no-op repaints, §7.2) — so the signal is
almost free: **a full sweep whose re-derivation changes a snapshot the watcher
never marked dirty is a dropped event, and it is measurable right there.**
Count it, attribute it (which root, which kind), and surface it. The same holds
for the cheap sweep's reconcile finding a workspace the enumeration roots did
not announce.

Per ~/AGENTS.md, do not add a stored counter if a query will do, and do not
build an index — this is instrumentation, not a metrics system. `ops.jsonl` and
the §11 activity accessory are the existing surfaces; prefer feeding them to
minting a fourth.

### 2. Simulate and prove

Build the scenarios that actually produce drift, as tests, not as prose:

- inotify watch-descriptor exhaustion (`fs.inotify.max_user_watches`) — the
  classic, and yog watches per workspace root.
- a workspace created/removed while the watch set is being reconciled.
- atomic-rename sequences (`git` writes refs this way constantly) — the watcher
  claims to coalesce these; prove it.
- a workspace on a filesystem inotify does not cover (NFS, overlayfs, a
  container bind mount) — `RecommendedWatcher` silently degrades to polling on
  some platforms and not others.
- `git gc` / pack rewrites under a watched `repo.git`.
- the nested-world roots (§16) — `LERNIE_HOME` and `XDG_STATE_HOME` are
  overridden under `$XDG_DATA_HOME/yog/world`; confirm every root that matters
  is actually watched and none is reached only by sweep.

For each: does the watcher catch it? If not, that is a **bug with a name**, and
it gets its own ball. The deliverable of this task is that list, proven.

### 3. Re-decide the sweeps

Once the drift sources are known and fixed, the sweeps should be justifiable on
their own terms or removed. The operator's position is that a change should be
picked up automatically and a sweep that catches something is evidence of a
defect. A sweep that provably catches nothing across the scenario suite is
either dead weight (delete it — AGENTS.md: subtraction) or a deliberate,
documented backstop whose catches are alarms rather than routine. Say which,
and write the reasoning into §7.2 — which today presents the 15 s sweep as a
latency bound rather than as a symptom.

Note the cheap sweep also does liveness re-probing, which is a poll of process
state, not of the filesystem — a different justification that may well survive
when the filesystem one does not. Separate them in the analysis.

## Acceptance

- A scenario suite that produces each drift source deliberately, with the
  watcher's behaviour asserted per scenario.
- Instrumentation that names a dropped event when one happens, reachable by the
  operator without a debugger.
- One ball filed per real defect found.
- §7.2 rewritten to say what the sweeps are FOR, given the evidence.

## Do not

Do not delete the sweeps before the scenarios prove them unnecessary. The
self-healing is currently load-bearing precisely because the defects are
unknown; removing the cover before fixing what it covers turns a hidden problem
into a visible outage.
