bl-4263 landed the engine end; here is the contract this client mirrors, byte for byte (REMOTE §13.2 as amended). MATERIAL an entry holds: the engine rendezvous public key (32 bytes ed25519) and the pairing salt (32 bytes), beside ca.pem. DERIVATIONS, all HKDF-SHA256 with salt "yog rendezvous" over the pairing salt, one info label each, 32 bytes out: "presence salt" (the DHT salt presence is filed under), "inbox salt", "seal key" (ChaCha20-Poly1305), "inbox key" (an ed25519 SEED — the inbox is signed under a keypair derived from the salt, so the client mints no keypair of its own for it). PRESENCE: BEP 44 get(engine public key, presence salt); the value is nonce(12) || ciphertext || tag(16) under the seal key, plaintext = endpoint list: a count byte, then per endpoint a family byte (4 or 6), the raw address bytes (4 or 16), a big-endian u16 port. The engine publishes its route-local addresses at its punch port (the observed endpoint is bl-efae), republished hourly with a rising seq. CALL (the inbox): put(inbox keypair signs, inbox salt, seq rising per write, value sealed the same way) with plaintext = 8-byte big-endian nonce then an endpoint list — the client punch port at the addresses it can be reached on. The engine polls every 15 s, remembers the last nonce it punched, and punches ONLY a call that verifies and unseals; a fresh nonce is one punch. PUNCH: bind one port with SO_REUSEADDR+SO_REUSEPORT, listen on it per family, and connect from the same port toward every engine endpoint, v6 first, one SYN per 2 s for a 20 s window; the engine does the same toward the call endpoints. Keep the FIRST stream you get — the engine serves every stream that lands, so whichever one you speak on is answered. WIRE over the stream: the ordinary inner mTLS as a client (ca.pem, your leaf, verify the engine name), the §3 preface, request frames as always. HELD: between asks the engine writes {"ping":true} as a frame every 25 s of silence — discard it wherever you read, it is never the start of a reply stream — and hangs up after 2 min of total silence, so a client that wants the connection held answers nothing and simply stays.

thrall half LANDED: thrall bl-0a8b, squash 60162ba on thrall origin/main (the app half is not this comment's).

What the foot now does, against the contract above byte for byte: an entry may carry `rendezvous.pub` (engine ed25519 public key, hex) and `pairing.salt` beside `ca.pem` — both or neither, neither is today's entry. The four HKDF derivations, a presence and a call the engine's own code sealed, and a signed item with its target are held as byte fixtures in thrall's suite, so interoperation is asserted rather than assumed. Every dial climbs the ladder — live held connection, direct `address` under a 10 s connect bound, re-punch at the RAM-cached engine endpoints, full rendezvous (presence get, sealed call put under the salt-derived inbox keypair with a rising seq, simultaneous open from one SO_REUSEADDR/SO_REUSEPORT port, v6 first, first stream kept). A punched connection is held across asks with the preface exchanged once; `{"ping":true}` is discarded wherever a reply stream is read; two minutes of socket silence hangs up as the wire. Redial (thrall bl-916d's loop) re-enters the whole ladder on every dial, so a cellular flap costs a punch window. thrall carries its own bencode/KRPC/BEP 44 client (ruling 2), loopback-fake tested, never a node; `ring` named directly, `socket2 =0.6.5` per ruling 1; the lockfile moved rustls to 0.23.45 past RUSTSEC-2026-0285, which was red on thrall's main gate before this ball. DESIGN §3.11 + module-map rows record it.

Three things stated as deviations or deferrals, each with its reason:
1. `address` stays REQUIRED on a roving entry: it is the engine NAME's one home for the inner mTLS (§13.3 "verifying the same engine name a direct dial verifies"), and thrall has no second file to read a name from. An unroutable address costs one bounded connect per dial, not a channel.
2. The 2-minute hangup is a socket read bound shortened by the suite, not an injected clock: rustls has no clean resume from a half-read record (yog's own IDLE_TIMEOUT note), so a clock-driven poll loop would need exactly the resume rustls lacks. The constant is asserted at 2 min; the mechanism is driven at 300 ms against a silent punched stand-in.
3. The client window is 35 s (engine poll 15 s + engine window 20 s), not 20 s: a client that stopped at 20 misses the engine's whole window when the poll comes late. Stated as a default to revisit on evidence (§13.7 ruling 3). A hand-off longer than the engine's 2 min costs the held connection; the capture rides the next dial via REMOTE §5.6, unchanged.

Not done here: no live walk against the mainline from a foot (bl-5d8d's class), no measurement of TCP simultaneous open on a real NAT pair (§13.8 still open), and the foot does not keep the engine awake through a long tool.

5. Filed yog-android bl-2990: the app's own rxing reader fails to read back some symbols its own encoder draws near 1650-1700 bytes, found while moving the level-M test to the roving mint's size.
