Measured 2026-09-28 on the first live punch attempt (bl-65dc). A seat on a laptop that uses the engine's box as its overlay exit node: punch::local_ips (the source address toward a public destination) answers the overlay's own addresses, and the DHT-observed address is the engine box's public address because the seat's UDP leaves through it. The call therefore lists (overlay v4, overlay v6, engine's own public IP) at the seat's punch port. The engine read the call, opened it and punched within a second — toward addresses the overlay drops for engine-initiated connections (verified: SYN-SENT from the punch port on the engine, zero SYNs arriving on the seat; a plain connect and a ping from the engine box to the seat's overlay address also fail while the reverse works) and toward its own router. Nothing in yog is wrong; the design assumption REMOTE §13.3 rests on — a client advertises addresses that are really its own — does not hold under an exit node.

Deliver: (1) REMOTE §13.8 records the trap as measured and what it means for the operator: a client tunnelled through the engine's own box needs no punch (its direct address works), and a client tunnelled through a third box publishes that box's address and cannot be punched; the overlay's ACLs decide engine→client reachability separately. (2) Decide, with the evidence, whether local_ips should skip tunnel interfaces (tun/wg/tailscale, point-to-point, non-routable-to-peer) in all four repos, or whether the ladder should rank the direct address ahead of a rendezvous whenever the call would carry only overlay addresses — prefer the reframe that dissolves the case over a per-interface list. (3) Whatever is chosen, the seat should refuse to spend a rendezvous it can predict cannot land, with a sentence naming why, rather than a 35 s silent window.
