## The gap

`yog tool-host` fans out one thread per channel — the flat root plus one per
entry (`src/wire/host/entries.rs`, bl-4e31) — and each thread rides a
follow-class read for its next invocation. The landed design says a thread that
died in any way is just a dropped sender: no arm, no state of the world.

That is the right *error* model and the wrong *lifetime* model for the roaming
case REMOTE §1 calls canonical. A laptop that sleeps, changes networks, or
crosses a tailnet relay switch drops TCP; the thread dies cleanly; and from
then until the process is restarted, that engine believes the tool host is
gone (presence is connection RAM, correctly) while the tool host process sits
there believing it is serving. The operator's symptom is tools that silently
stopped being offered, with a live-looking process.

## The shape of the fix, and what it must not be

A redial loop with backoff around each channel's thread — the channel dies,
the loop waits, the channel is dialled again, presence re-forms on the engine
side exactly as a fresh connection would. Nothing protocol-side changes:
advertisement already rides the connection, registration is durable
server-side, and the invocation mailbox already tolerates a consumer coming
and going (REMOTE §5).

It must NOT become a session-resume mechanism: there is nothing to resume —
lernie bans the word, presence is deliberately ephemeral, and an invocation
that was in flight when the connection died is the mailbox's problem, not the
redial's. It also must not spin: a box with no route (airplane mode) should
settle to a slow cadence, not burn a core.

The seat side needs nothing — seat verbs dial per gesture and the window asker
polls, so both self-heal by construction. The follow lane (bl-73e7) should be
checked for the same property while here: a held read that dies mid-stream
should surface as the pull asker's next answer, not as a wedge.
