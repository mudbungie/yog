Round 1, devadmin lane. Provisioning a foot on a second machine, end to end.

`yog wire-certs --help` documents exactly one way to give another box a leaf:

    `WIRE_LEAF=<common-name>` asks for the other act instead: issue ONE extra
    client leaf under that name, over the CA already here — no CA, no address,
    no other leaf. That is the leaf a visiting box participates as; carry it,
    its key and `ca.pem` to that box by hand and place them in its
    `wire/workspaces/<workspace>/` as `client.pem`, `client.key` and `ca.pem`,
    beside an `address` naming this engine.

Followed verbatim (plus `WIRE_FOOT=1`, which that page does not mention — its
own ball), the box dials, the mTLS handshake succeeds, the foot advertises,
and NOTHING HAPPENS. thrall prints not one line and parks on its mailbox read.
On the engine:

    $ lernie clients ops
    {"kind":"clients","ok":true,"rows":[{"client":"yog-client",...}]}

The foot is not in the roster. On disk the advertisement DID land:

    <world>/state/yog/clients/devbox/tools.json     # the advertised set, stored
    # no <world>/state/yog/clients/devbox/workspaces/ at all

So the client exists, its tools are stored, and it is registered in no
workspace — REMOTE 5.1 says an advertisement presents into the workspaces the
client is registered in, and that set is empty. It presented into nothing. No
sentence anywhere says so: not to the foot, not on the engine`s stderr, not in
the roster, not on the ops trail.

Then the repair is refused:

    $ lernie enroll ops devbox foot
    ~/.../wire already holds devbox.pem or devbox.key: re-issuing distrusts
    nothing, so both would be live under one identity. One device is one name
    and one leaf, whatever its grade — ... State another common name, or
    rotate the whole directory with FORCE=1

Good sentence, wrong situation. `/enroll` is the only act that registers, it
refuses because `wire-certs` already made the leaf, and the alternative it
offers (`FORCE=1`) rotates the CA and distrusts every certificate already
carried to every other box.

The operator is now wedged with a valid, connectable, useless foot and no verb
that fixes it. The escape found was to create the registration file by hand in
the engine`s private state root.

EXPECTED (any one of these closes it):
- `wire-certs WIRE_LEAF=` says, in its success text, that the leaf is
  registered in no workspace and names the act that registers it;
- an advertisement that lands in zero workspaces answers the foot with that
  fact rather than `{"kind":"advertised","ok":true}` — a foot that offers
  nothing to nobody should be told;
- `/enroll` on a name whose leaf already exists registers the existing leaf
  instead of refusing (registration is not re-issue and distrusts nothing);
- the roster lists a registered-nowhere client so the operator can SEE it.

Severity p2: it is the documented enrolment path for the exact case the
component exists for (a second machine), it fails silently at both ends, and
its only stated remedy is a CA rotation.
