bl-f297 closed the read-path residual's two soft classes and left two hard ones, each now blocked on named work rather than on an undecided question. REMOTE 9.7 carries the reasoning in full; this is the work.

1. THE FOLD CLASS — the answer moves altitude.

Query::Conversations answers the all-collapsed list and the frame paints the expanded one; the whole section 11 inspector family (transcript, steps, files, step detail, rail, inbox) folds through the rail PIN, and no query spells a pinned Files listing at all.

bl-f297 attacked three shapes and ruled on all three. The fold as a query PARAMETER is refused on DESIGN 8.5's own sentence — views gain no boundary representation, by design — and the selection-carrying queries beside it (Files{path}, Step{seq}, WorkDiff{file}) are not a counter-example: a selection names which thing you are asking about, a fold names which answers you have open. The fold as a DURABLE pane fact is already closed by DESIGN 5.3's entry on the expanded set. What is left is the one that works: the boundary answers the whole descent FOREST with its per-row rollups, all-collapsed being the root subset of it, and each seat renders the rows its own fold makes visible. The fold never crosses, the seat selects rather than derives, and one derivation still serves both seats.

That is a payload change to Reply::Conversations that every seat and the codec read — a headless /conversations included — plus the same move on Reply::Rail for the pin, plus the pinned Files listing that has no query. It is a payload ball, not a read-path one. Attack the pin half separately before building: four tabs read through one pin, so the question is whether the pin is a cut applied to a complete answer or six queries that each take one.

2. THE ADDRESSING CLASS — focus by name.

Reply::Workspaces carries no path (8.1's ruling) and nav::tabs::Tab holds the PathBuf a click, a pin toggle and the 3.6 delete seat all spend. The shape to REFUSE is the cheap one: painting off a reply while resolving each name through the snapshot's own name/path table at the seat's door is two sources for one fact, and worse than not migrating. The honest change is focus held by NAME, resolved at the doors that need a path — the same shape Prepared::binding waits on, and affordable for the same reason: once a seat's read path is the only read path.

3. SEARCH, off the unblocked list.

bl-adcb called search unblocked; that reading predates 9.8. The asker's pass is serial over the standing set, and 9.8 put acts on their own thread because a long gesture would stall every standing read. Query::Search walks every transcript in the world and a standing question is re-asked every ask period, so riding the asker turns a once-per-ask walk into a 2 Hz one AND puts it in front of every other surface's answer. Two shapes remain, neither of which retires a mechanism: a second lane on the asker (which is the poster, renamed), or point the existing Searcher thread at the wire — it stays a thread, justified by exactly that argument, and what changes is that its read crosses the boundary instead of running in process. The second is smaller and is the one to take; the win is 1.2 compliance, not one fewer thread.

4. THE CONFIG EDITORS, which were never one of the three classes.

Of the five queries the bl-adcb audit filed under the config family, exactly one was ever a frame-side read (marks, migrated in bl-f297). The 9.1/9.2/9.3 editors do not reach ReadConfig/Lineages/Models/Providers at all — they load their bytes directly, and their writes are the boundary's. Pointing them at those queries replaces Editor::load with an answer, which is a change to what a config editor IS (9.5's controls over facts), not a read moved onto a wire. Rule on that before building it.

Not in scope: the four remaining acts (bl-1747), and Prepared::binding.
