REMOTE §8.2 (bl-aaec): the window attaches to every channel it holds — the
loopback engine plus one channel per entry, each on the entry's own
material.

Needs bl-028a (the channel/slice composition).

What to build (pre-split `src/engine/window/channels.rs` —
`src/engine/window.rs` is ~170 lines and this projects past 200):

- `Engine::window_wire` builds the channel set: the loopback channel
  exactly as today (`window_seat`'s RAM-address hand-over and forced
  `loopback()` are one channel's address resolution now — REMOTE §8.2's
  reframe verdict, half taken half rejected), plus one channel per
  `wire::entries::entries(world)` row.
- One asker per channel (`src/wire/asker.rs` parameterized by channel),
  each feeding its slice at `ASK_PERIOD`. `Asker::seat_window` runs ONLY
  on the loopback channel — the engine seats its OWN window leaf (REMOTE
  §4.1); a remote registration is the host operator's file and nothing
  here writes one.
- The poster routes each act by the workspace it names to that channel; an
  act naming no workspace goes to loopback. Exactly-once per act is
  unchanged — routing picks a channel, never duplicates. This routing is
  also what makes REMOTE §7's amended fact-locality true for ACTS: a
  foreign workspace's seen/pin gestures land at its host.
- The follow lane dials the channel hosting the focused conversation's
  workspace; the searcher fans out over every channel and unions
  (superseded answers dropped on publish, as today).
- A channel whose Seat cannot open, or whose entry carried an Err, lands
  its sentence on its slice. Painting it is the next ball;
  `shell::refusal` (whole-shell) stays reserved for the loopback wire
  (bl-dc14).

Tests: two real listeners on one box — the `wire::tests` two-listener
pattern plus `test_support::wire` runtime mints — driving the window's
channel set against its own engine plus a second engine reached through an
entry; act routing by name; seat_window scoped to loopback.
