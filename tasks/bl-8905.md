Filed by bl-720a (the spec subtask of the expander epic bl-fa82), which was
fenced OUT of this decision by the operator — verbatim, on the altitude-1

spec recorded the fact and filed this; it touched nothing.

**The fact.** After bl-fa82 lands, the §11 conversation list (altitude 0)
renders the descent-id membership tree itself: every visible row is the
subtree rooted at its agent, a right-pinned ▶/▼ field expands a row's direct
children as indented rows, and clicking a child row focuses that member. That
is exactly what `src/shell/members.rs` — the altitude-1 "compact descent
tree", one indented selectable row per member of the *open* conversation —
already does, for one conversation, one altitude down.

Two renderings of one fact (§5.1 #8) is the drift risk the house rule names.
They already differ in what they carry: the member row wears the §6 marks,
`✉n` pending mail and a per-tool-call in-flight chip; the list row wears the
aggregated state badge, the flight class, the age, the ball badge, the
attention flag and now the subagent field. Neither is a superset.

**The decision this ball owes** (not made here):
- **Keep both** — they answer different questions (the list is *which
  conversation*, the tree is *which member of the one I am reading*) and the
  overlap is the price of two altitudes. Then say so in §11 and stop calling
  it a duplicate.
- **Retire the tree** — altitude 1's center reclaims the vertical space, and
  member selection is the list's job. Then the member row's marks / `✉n` /
  tool chips need a seat on the list row or they are lost.
- **Retire the list's expansion for the open conversation** — rejected on its
  face by bl-fa82's ruling; recorded only so the third door is visibly shut.

Whichever way it goes, the deliverable is the DESIGN §11 paragraph plus the
code that matches it. Read bl-fa82's body first: both operator rulings are
quoted there verbatim.
